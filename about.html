About Flyback Bronze

An honest guide for people who love how watches work

We explain mechanical watches the way a patient friend at the workbench would — clearly, without jargon, and without trying to sell you anything.

Our story

Why we started Flyback Bronze

Watches can be surprisingly hard to learn about. Much of what you find is either deeply technical, written for collectors who already know the vocabulary, or quietly shaped by advertising. If you simply want to know how your automatic watch winds itself, why your bronze case is turning brown, or whether you can swim with it, the answers are often buried.

Flyback Bronze exists to change that. We write practical, independent guides for curious owners and first-time buyers alike, and we build small interactive tools — a flyback demo, a fit guide, a water-resistance decoder — that make ideas easier to grasp.

The name joins two things we find fascinating. The flyback chronograph is a clever piece of engineering designed to make a pilot's life easier. Bronze is a material that changes with its owner, telling a story over years of wear. Together they capture what we love: mechanics and character.

What we stand for

Three principles behind every guide

I

Independent

We don't sell watches, accept sponsored placements or earn commissions from brands. Our only interest is giving you clear, accurate information.

II

Practical

Every guide answers real questions owners ask, with steps you can follow at home and clear advice on when to see a professional watchmaker.

III

Original

All our writing is our own. We use licensed photography, respect trademarks and credit sources where facts come from elsewhere.

How we write

From the workbench to plain English

Our guides start with established watchmaking knowledge and manufacturers' published specifications. We explain concepts using everyday comparisons, test our interactive tools carefully, and review pages when standards or common practice change.

We never pretend a guide can replace a qualified watchmaker. Where a job needs specialist tools — opening a caseback, replacing seals, regulating a movement — we say so clearly and point you towards professional help.

Who we're for

First-time buyers trying to understand the difference between quartz and automatic. Owners of a new bronze watch wondering what's happening to the case. Collectors who want a clear reference to share with friends. And anyone who has ever looked at a watch and wondered how it works. Everything on this site is free to read.
